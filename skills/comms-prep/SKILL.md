# Comms Prep — prepare before drafting

Prepare when the substance is ready to communicate. If underlying work must happen first, finish
that work; if the task is writing, preparation belongs at the start. The user's requested scope
and form take precedence over these defaults.

## Choose the depth

- **Routine reply or small edit with low consequences:** consider who is reading, what they need
  to understand or do, what you might falsely assume they know, what source meaning must survive,
  and what would mislead them. Use those answers directly, without a written brief or additional
  references. Draft and self-review for reader fit, source fidelity, and applicable style; fix
  concrete defects and recheck changed meaning.
- **Substantial artifact or consequential message:** prepare or reuse a short written brief. When
  creating or materially revising it, read [Written brief](references/brief.md). It covers objective,
  audience and knowledge, source meaning, form, style, and the review decision. A short approval
  request or customer commitment may need more care than a long informal reply.
- **Existing preparation:** reuse it while the objective, audience, sources, form, and style still
  fit. Amend what materially changes. Routine follow-ups can use the quick check. If a draft lacks
  preparation, start from its request and intended purpose rather than adopting the draft's assumptions.

## Audience and source judgement

The reader's own terminology is strongest evidence of familiarity, followed by task context and
ordinary knowledge for an evidenced role. Keep reasonable assumptions distinct from observations;
a role alone does not establish knowledge of local labels or project history. Introduce useful
unfamiliar terms in the reader's words. Carry the same evidence and limits into audience review.

Verify claims against the sources available for the task. Preserve consequential asks, order,
distinctions, exclusions, contradictions, and uncertainty when rewriting. Check available sources
for missing facts and resolve routine wording choices within scope. Ask only when a material fact
or choice requires someone else's input and cannot be handled honestly by narrowing the claim;
continue unaffected work. An unanswered preparation question alone is not a reason to stop.

## Choose review and complete the writing

Every draft gets self-review. For long, public, or consequential communications, also use
`/comms-review` with independent audience review. Use that depth when explicitly requested or when
hidden context or unresolved reader confusion makes a fresh perspective valuable. Record the
choice and reason in a written brief; reconsider if scope or consequences materially change.

When writing is requested, continue from preparation through drafting and the chosen review to
the finished communication. Preparation alone completes the task only when that was the request.
Apply the task's house style. Keep briefs and review notes in private working context or scratch
space, outside delivered files and messages unless the user requests them.
